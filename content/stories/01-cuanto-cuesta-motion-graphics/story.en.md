# How much does a motion graphics video cost?

::scene s01-still

## Why nobody gives you a price right away

Search "how much does a motion graphics video cost" and you'll find everything: per-second rates, fixed
packages and studios that simply say "it depends". It does depend. What almost nobody explains is **on
what**.

Asking for the price of an animated video with no other details is like asking for the price of a house
without saying how many rooms it has. A five-second animated logo and a two-minute character explainer
share the same craft, but not the same amount of work. Between one and the other, the hours of design,
animation, review and audio production change completely.

That's why MOVO doesn't publish fixed prices: every project is quoted by its length, its complexity and the
number of pieces. What we can do is show you how a quote comes together, so you arrive at the conversation
knowing what to ask for and what to expect. That's what this story is about.

## It isn't a number: it's a set of decisions

::scene s01-cover

The cost of an animated video doesn't come from a rate card: it comes from the decisions you make about it.
Each one tips the scale toward a simpler, faster piece or toward a more elaborate one.

The good news is that most of those decisions are yours. Once you know what they are, you can fit the
project to your budget without giving up what matters: a video that does its job.

We group the variables into four blocks. **Length**: how many seconds the piece runs. **Style**: how complex
the animation is. **Formats**: how many sizes and versions you get. **Extras**: script, voice, music,
revisions and timing. Let's go through each one.

## The decisions that move the price

### 1. Length

It's the most obvious variable, but not the one that matters most. Every second of animation has to be
designed, animated and reviewed, so a 60-second video takes more work than a 15-second one. For reference:

- **Animated logo:** 3 to 6 seconds.
- **Social ads:** 6, 15 and 30-second versions.
- **Explainer video:** 60 to 120 seconds.

An honest tip: longer isn't better. On social, what matters is said in the first few seconds. A shorter,
sharper video usually outperforms a long, generic one.

### 2. Style and complexity

This is where quotes differ the most. Two 30-second videos can require very different work:

| Style | What it is | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic type | Text, shapes and graphics moving to a rhythm | Low |
| Graphics & illustration | Illustrated scenes, icons, data and transitions | Medium |
| Characters | Illustration with characters who act and emote | High |
| 3D | Products, spaces or objects modeled and animated in three dimensions | High |

No style is better than another: there's the right one for your message, your audience and your brand. Hard
data reads best as graphics; an emotional story, with characters.

### 3. Script and concept

Do you have a script, or do you need us to write it? Both work. If your message is already clear, the
project starts faster. If not, we define the message and map it frame by frame in a storyboard before
animating. That step prevents the most expensive thing in any video: redoing animation because the idea
changed halfway through.

### 4. Voice-over and music

A video without sound loses half its impact. Our projects include licensed music and professional
voice-over, so you don't have to find or pay for licenses yourself. Before you ask for a quote, it helps to
know whether your video needs a voice or just music, and in how many languages.

### 5. Versions and formats

A video rarely lives in just one place. What plays horizontally on YouTube has to work vertically on Reels
and TikTok and square in the feed. A new format isn't just a crop: scenes have to be recomposed so nothing
important falls outside the frame.

- **Vertical 9:16:** Reels, TikTok, Shorts and Stories.
- **Square 1:1 and 4:5:** Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn feeds.
- **Horizontal 16:9:** YouTube, websites and presentations.
- **Custom:** LED screens, stages and booths.

If you're running ads, think about cutdowns too: a 6, a 15 and a 30-second version to test which one
performs. Each version adds work, but it multiplies how much you get out of the piece.

### 6. Review rounds

Revisions are part of the process, not an extra. MOVO includes two rounds of revisions at each key stage:
script, styleframes and animation. Reviewing at every stage is what keeps a project on time and on budget: a
script change costs little; the same change once everything is animated costs a lot more.

### 7. Delivery time and rush jobs

We can deliver finished productions in a couple of days; more complex projects take a couple of weeks. You
get a schedule with the proposal. If you need your video sooner than its complexity calls for, it can be
done, but it's planned differently: say so in your first message.

### 8. One-off piece or monthly plan

If you need a single video (a launch, an explainer, a campaign), it's quoted as a one-off piece. If your
brand posts all the time, a monthly plan makes more sense: a fixed set of 8, 12 or more animated pieces a
month, with a calendar, creative direction and a consistent look. Your brand always in motion, without
quoting every video.

## What to have ready for an accurate quote

::result

With these answers we'll send you a quote within 48 hours:

1. **Goal:** what should happen after someone watches the video? Sell, explain, launch, be remembered.
2. **Audience and channel:** who will watch it and where? Social, your website, a presentation, a screen at an event.
3. **Approximate length:** even a range helps.
4. **References:** two or three videos you like (and one you don't).
5. **Script or message:** if you have it, share it; if not, tell us the idea.
6. **Formats:** which sizes and how many versions you need.
7. **Date:** when you need the video live.
