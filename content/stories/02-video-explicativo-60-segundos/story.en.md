# How to explain a complex product in 60 seconds

::act
::scene s02-still

## The problem isn't your product: it's how much you want to say

Your product does a lot. It has features, integrations, use cases, advantages over the competition and a
story behind it. All of it is true and all of it matters. The trouble starts when you try to fit it all into
one video.

You can spot the result right away: a voice racing to name every feature, screens packed with text, arrows,
icons and music that never lets anything breathe. Viewers are lost within ten seconds. Not because your
product is hard, but because the video asked them to understand ten things at once.

An explainer video isn't an animated spec sheet. It's something else: a short story that makes someone
understand **one idea** well enough to want to know more. Your website, your sales team or your demo do the
rest.

::act
## One idea per video, one idea per scene

::scene s02-cover

Before writing a single line, answer one question: if viewers remembered only one sentence, what would it
be? That sentence is the video. Anything that doesn't support it goes.

With a clear idea, 60 seconds is plenty. A good explainer usually splits its minute like this:

| Moment | Approximate time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | first 5 seconds | Names the problem in your customer's words |
| Problem | up to second 20 | Shows what it costs not to solve it |
| Solution | up to second 50 | Your product in action: how it works, not every feature |
| Close | last 10 seconds | What your customer gains and what to do next |

The rule we apply to every scene is the same as for the whole video: one scene, one idea. If a scene needs
to explain two things, it's two scenes. That way the pace carries itself and nobody gets lost.

### 60 or 120 seconds?

Our explainer videos run 60 to 120 seconds, and the length is set by where the video is used, not by how
many things your product does:

- **Around 60 seconds** for your website, a campaign or a first contact: someone who doesn't know you yet and
  is giving you a minute.
- **Up to 120 seconds** for onboarding, training or a sales presentation: someone who has already decided to
  listen and needs to understand how it works.
- **A short social cut**, included with the final video: the hook and the main idea in a few seconds, so
  the explainer also works on Instagram, TikTok or LinkedIn.

If your product has several important features, instead of one four-minute video, a series of short videos
works better, one per feature. Each one stands on its own, and together they tell the whole story.

::act
## How an explainer video is built

An explainer isn't animated in one go. It's built in layers, and each layer is approved before moving to the
next. This is how we work at MOVO, in five stages:

::scene s02-layers

### 1. Brief

We learn your goal, your audience and where the video will live: your website, a sales deck, a campaign or
your customer onboarding. Explaining your product to a buyer isn't the same as explaining it to a new user.

### 2. Script & storyboard

We write the message and map it frame by frame before animating. The script is where we decide what stays
and what goes; the storyboard, how each idea looks. It's the most important stage: this is where a change
costs little.

### 3. Styleframes

We design the video's key frames so you approve the look —colors, type, illustration style— before anything
moves.

### 4. Animation

We bring it to life. Motion isn't decoration: it guides the eye, sets the pace and makes something complex
make sense in order, one step at a time.

### 5. Delivery

You get the final video with music and a short social cut, in each platform's formats.

At every key stage —script, styleframes and animation— we include two rounds of revisions. Reviewing layer by
layer avoids the most expensive surprise: finding out at the end that the idea was the wrong one.

## What makes an explainer work

After many videos, some patterns keep showing up in the ones people actually understand:

- **Talk about the problem before the product.** Nobody cares about a solution until they recognize their
  problem.
- **Show, don't list.** An interface in motion explains more than a list of features. For software and apps,
  animated interface walkthroughs teach how it's used without a live demo.
- **Use your customer's words, not your team's.** If your audience doesn't use an acronym, neither should
  your video.
- **Let it breathe.** Half a second of pause after an important idea is worth more than another line of text.
- **End with one action.** Book a demo, get in touch, download the app. One, not three.

An example: for [LABS](/en/work/labs-caso-estudio-inmobiliaria/) we told, in under a minute, how an AI agent
on WhatsApp helped a real estate developer answer every lead on time. A complex product —AI, automation,
integration— boiled down to one clear idea: technology opens the door, people close the sale.

And explaining isn't only for products. For [Viviendo Vino](/en/work/viviendo-vino-botellas-750-ml/) we
answered a curious question in one minute —why are almost all wine bottles 750 ml?— made for social. Same
logic, different goal: keep people watching to the end and get them to share it.
